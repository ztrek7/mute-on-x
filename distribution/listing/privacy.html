Mute on X privacy policy

Mute on X runs locally in your browser on x.com. It has no developer-operated collection service, advertising, or analytics, and it does not sell data. It does not ask for your X password.

Muted words

When you right-click, the extension remembers the word under the pointer or the selected text in that page’s memory. A capture can be used once. It expires after one minute, and it is dropped when you leave the page. Password fields are ignored.

When you choose Mute on X, an inactive tab opens X’s muted-word settings. The extension enters the term in X’s form and checks the reloaded list. X receives the term through your existing sign-in. You can edit or remove the entry in X’s settings. The extension does not save the muted term in its own storage.

While a mute is in progress, temporary session storage holds the originating x.com page URL and identifiers for the page, tab, window, and request. The extension uses these records to show a result on the original page and to close its temporary tab. It removes a request’s record when the request finishes or is cleaned up after an interruption. An alarm wakes that cleanup.

Feed filters

You can turn on four filters: hide posts from non-US accounts, also hide unverified locations, hide identifiable non-English text, and hide posts that open in all caps. They start off. The language check reads post text and author names in the page. The all-caps check reads the first sentence of the post. That text is not sent anywhere.

When “Hide posts from non-US accounts” is on, the extension requests the account-location label for authors in the feed. The request is a read-only GET to X’s About account operation on x.com or api.x.com, one author at a time. It uses your existing X session. It reads the ct0 cookie and sends that value only to X, as the CSRF header, with the public client identifier already used by x.com. The cookie and the location result are not sent to the extension’s author.

The location request includes the author’s account handle, including a quoted author when applicable. X receives the request with the browser’s X credentials. The extension does not persist the CSRF value. X controls the records it keeps for requests and muted words.

What is stored

Local storage keeps the four filter switches, a count of posts blocked over time, timestamps used to pace location requests, a cooldown reason, and up to 400 location results. Those results are stored under a hash of an install-specific salt, the viewer, and the author. The salt is kept in the same local record. Account names are not the on-disk keys.

Session storage keeps recent location results for the open feed, keyed by account name or by account name plus a numeric id. In ordinary windows it also keeps the numeric ids of posts already counted in the current browser session. A private window keeps its location cache in session storage and its counted post ids in a private database that the browser removes when the last private window closes. Ordinary and private windows do not share this data.

The filter switches also apply in private windows when the user allows the extension there. Confirmed classifications are eligible for reuse for 30 minutes by handle, or 24 hours when a numeric account id matches. Missing labels are normally cached for ten minutes in session storage. Temporary failures and cooldowns use shorter or server-directed retry times. An unidentified viewer gets document-scoped results. In regular windows, the rolling request budget considers the past hour and rate-limit deadlines can persist across restarts. Expired entries stop affecting lookups and are pruned on subsequent writes. Preferences and the regular all-time count persist until the extension’s local data is cleared or the extension is removed. Removing the extension does not remove muted words from X.

Permissions

The extension declares the context menu, storage, and alarms permissions. Its page script runs only on https://x.com/. A private window uses a separate copy of the extension.
